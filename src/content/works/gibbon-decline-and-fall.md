synopsis: >-
  Gibbon's history in six volumes covers thirteen centuries, from the empire at its height under the Antonines in the second century to the fall of Constantinople in 1453. The first half follows the emperors, the barbarian invasions and the rise of Christianity to the end of the western empire in 476; the second covers Justinian, Islam, the Crusades and Byzantium. Its famous fifteenth and sixteenth chapters, on the growth of the early Church, treat Christianity as a historical cause of Rome's decline and drew furious replies.
