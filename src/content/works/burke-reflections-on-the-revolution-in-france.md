synopsis: >-
  Written as a letter to a young Frenchman a year after the fall of the Bastille, Burke's attack on the Revolution predicts that it will end in military dictatorship. He argues that a society is a partnership between the dead, the living and the unborn; that rights are inherited, not deduced from abstract principle; that prejudice and habit hold civilisation together; and that the Assembly's confiscation of church property and destruction of the old orders has let loose forces it cannot govern. The passage on Marie Antoinette and the age of chivalry is the most famous.
