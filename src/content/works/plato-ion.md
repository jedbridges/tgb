synopsis: >-
  Socrates meets Ion, a professional reciter of Homer fresh from winning a prize, and asks how he can speak so well about Homer and not about other poets. Ion cannot say. Socrates suggests that rhapsode and poet alike work not by knowledge or skill but by divine possession, passed down like the pull of a magnet through a chain of iron rings. Ion, unable to name any subject on which he is the expert, accepts the label of inspired rather than admit to being a fraud.
