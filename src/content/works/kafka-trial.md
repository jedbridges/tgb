  Josef K., a bank official, is arrested on the morning of his thirtieth birthday without being told what he is accused of. He is left free to go about his life while his case proceeds in a court that sits in tenement attics and whose law no one has read. He hires a lawyer, seeks help from a painter and a priest, and hears the parable Before the Law, but never learns the charge. A year later two men in frock coats lead him to a quarry and kill him. Kafka left the novel unfinished; Max Brod published it.
