synopsis: >-
  Berkeley argues that matter does not exist. Nothing is real except minds and their ideas, and to be is either to perceive or to be perceived. The ordinary things we call bodies are collections of sensible qualities, and since those qualities exist only in perception, so do the things. An introduction attacks abstract general ideas as the source of philosophical confusion, and Locke's distinction between primary and secondary qualities is dismantled. The regularity of what we perceive is then explained by God, who keeps the world in being by perceiving it and presents our sensations in lawful order.
