synopsis: >-
  Tocqueville's last book, written from the archives of the intendants, argues that the French Revolution completed work the monarchy had already begun. Centralisation, the destruction of local liberty, the reduction of the nobility to a caste with privileges but no function, the separation of classes and the growing equality of Frenchmen under a single administration were all in place before 1789. What the Revolution did was to sweep away the debris and finish the job, which is why the administrative state emerged from it stronger than before. Along the way he explains why the peasants who owned land resented the old taxes most, and why reform made revolt more likely.
