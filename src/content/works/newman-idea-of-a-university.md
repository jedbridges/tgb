synopsis: >-
  Nine discourses Newman delivered in Dublin in 1852 when he was asked to found a Catholic university there, later joined to a set of lectures and essays on university subjects. He argues that a university exists to teach universal knowledge, that theology is a branch of knowledge and cannot be excluded without distorting the rest, that knowledge is its own end, and that a liberal education forms the philosophical habit of mind and the gentleman rather than the professional. The fifth discourse, Knowledge Its Own End, is the most often read.
