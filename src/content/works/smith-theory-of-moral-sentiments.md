synopsis: >-
  Smith's first book, and the one he revised until his death, is a theory of how moral judgment works. Its foundation is sympathy, our capacity to imagine ourselves in another's situation and to feel a version of what they feel. We approve of conduct when we can go along with it, and we judge ourselves by imagining an impartial spectator looking on. From this Smith derives accounts of propriety, merit, justice, the sense of duty, the corrupting admiration of the rich and the great, and the character of the virtuous person, and surveys the systems of moral philosophy before him.
