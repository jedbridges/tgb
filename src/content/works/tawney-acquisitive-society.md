synopsis: >-
  An essay by an English economic historian and Christian socialist, expanded from a Fabian pamphlet. Tawney argues that modern industrial society is acquisitive, organised around rights to property and income without regard to the functions they serve, and that this makes it both unjust and unstable. He proposes instead a functional society in which industry is treated as a profession, ownership is tied to service, and the standard of conduct is the purpose an activity serves rather than the wealth it yields. Chapters treat property, the nostrum of productivity, the vice of the industrial system and the position of the brain worker.
