synopsis: >-
  One of the three essays appended to the Discourse on Method, and the founding text of analytic geometry. Descartes shows that the arithmetical operations can be performed on line segments, so that curves can be represented by equations in two unknowns and geometrical problems solved algebraically. Book one treats problems solvable with circles and straight lines. Book two classifies curves by the degree of their equations and finds their normals and tangents. Book three applies the algebra to the roots of equations and the classical problems of doubling the cube and trisecting the angle.
