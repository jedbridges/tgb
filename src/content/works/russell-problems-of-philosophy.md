synopsis: >-
  A short introduction to philosophy that begins with a table and asks what we can really know about it. Russell distinguishes appearance from reality, sense-data from physical objects, knowledge by acquaintance from knowledge by description, and argues that induction and the existence of universals must be accepted as self-evident. Later chapters examine idealism, a priori knowledge, and the limits of philosophical knowledge, ending with a defence of philosophy for the questions it raises rather than the answers it gives.
