synopsis: >-
  The second of Nietzsche's four Untimely Meditations, written when he was a young professor of classical philology. He argues that the nineteenth century suffers from an excess of historical knowledge that paralyses action, and that history serves life in three ways, monumental, antiquarian and critical, each of which becomes poisonous when it dominates. Life requires a horizon and the power to forget. The essay ends by calling on youth to resist the historical culture of the universities.
