  In a shabby Paris boarding house in 1819, the young law student Eugène de Rastignac learns how society works. His fellow lodger Goriot, a retired pasta merchant, has beggared himself for two daughters who have married into the aristocracy and are ashamed of him; another lodger, Vautrin, is an escaped convict who offers Rastignac a shortcut to wealth by murder. Goriot dies abandoned by his daughters, and Rastignac, who alone attends the burial, looks down on Paris from the cemetery and challenges it. The novel that launched Balzac's Human Comedy.
