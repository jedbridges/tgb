synopsis: >-
  Written in 44 BCE when Cicero was sixty-two and dedicated to his friend Atticus, the dialogue puts its argument in the mouth of Cato the Elder at eighty-four, talking with the young Scipio and Laelius. Cato answers four complaints against old age in turn, that it withdraws us from activity, weakens the body, takes away pleasure and brings death near. He cites old statesmen, farmers and philosophers, praises the pleasures of agriculture and conversation, and argues that death is either the end of sensation or the beginning of a better life, so that neither prospect should frighten a good man.
