synopsis: >-
  A poem of about four hundred and thirty lines in five parts, cut to its present shape with Ezra Pound's help. Fragments of speech, quotation and scene follow one another without connecting narrative: a woman remembering a sled ride in the mountains, a fortune teller's cards, crowds flowing over London Bridge, a nervous conversation in a rich room, a pub monologue about false teeth, a typist and a clerk, the burning of Carthage and the Buddha's fire sermon, drowned Phlebas the Phoenician, and a waterless journey to a chapel. Eliot's notes point to the Grail legend, Dante, Shakespeare and the Upanishads.
