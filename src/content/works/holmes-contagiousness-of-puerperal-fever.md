synopsis: >-
  A paper read to the Boston Society for Medical Improvement in 1843 by Holmes, then a young physician and not yet famous as a writer. Assembling reported cases in which one doctor or midwife attended a run of women who died of childbed fever while colleagues in the same town had none, he argued that the disease is carried from patient to patient on the hands and clothes of their attendants, and laid down rules for physicians, including staying away from midwifery after performing autopsies. The claim was attacked by leading obstetricians; Holmes reissued the paper in 1855, four years before Semmelweis published.
