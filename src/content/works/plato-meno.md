synopsis: >-
  The young Thessalian aristocrat Meno asks Socrates whether virtue can be taught. Socrates says he does not even know what virtue is, and the two fail to define it. When Meno objects that one cannot search for what one does not know, Socrates leads an uneducated slave boy to a geometrical truth and argues that learning is recollection of what the soul already knows. The dialogue ends by suggesting that virtue, if it is not knowledge, comes by a kind of divine gift.
