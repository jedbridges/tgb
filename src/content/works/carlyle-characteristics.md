synopsis: >-
  An essay written for the Edinburgh Review on the proposition that unconsciousness is the sign of health and self-examination the sign of disease. Carlyle argues that a sound body does not feel itself, that a sound faith does not argue about itself, and that the endless inquiry of his own age into its beliefs, its institutions and its literature is evidence of sickness rather than progress. He surveys the symptoms in philosophy, religion, politics and art, but refuses to end in despair: the pain of self-consciousness is the crisis of a cure, and a new faith is struggling to be born out of the wreck of the old.
