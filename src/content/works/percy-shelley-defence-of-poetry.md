synopsis: >-
  Written in 1821 in reply to Thomas Love Peacock's essay The Four Ages of Poetry, which had argued that poetry was obsolete in an age of science, and published after Shelley's death. Shelley argues that poetry in the broad sense, the imaginative making of order, underlies language, law, religion and science, and that poets have been the founders of civil society. Poetry enlarges the sympathetic imagination, which is the instrument of moral good. It closes by calling poets the unacknowledged legislators of the world.
