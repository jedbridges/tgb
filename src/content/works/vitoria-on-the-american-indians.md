synopsis: >-
  A lecture delivered at the University of Salamanca in 1539 by the Dominican theologian Francisco de Vitoria on the justice of Spain's conquest of the Americas. He argues that the Indians were the true owners of their lands before the Spaniards came, and rejects the claims of the emperor and the pope to universal dominion, the right of discovery, the refusal of the faith and the sins of the natives as just titles to conquest. He then sets out grounds on which Spanish rule might be lawful, such as the right to travel and trade and to defend the innocent. The lecture is a founding text of international law.
