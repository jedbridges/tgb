  The statement of principles adopted by the French National Assembly on 26 August 1789, six weeks after the fall of the Bastille, later placed at the head of the constitution of 1791. Its seventeen articles declare that men are born free and equal in rights, that the aim of political association is to preserve liberty, property, security and resistance to oppression, that sovereignty resides in the nation, that law is the expression of the general will, and that no one may be accused, arrested or punished except by law.
