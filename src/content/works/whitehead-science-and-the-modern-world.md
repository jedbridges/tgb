synopsis: >-
  Whitehead's Lowell Lectures, given in Boston in 1925, trace the rise of scientific thinking from its medieval and Greek origins through the century of genius, the seventeenth, to the Romantic reaction, relativity and quantum theory. He argues that modern science rests on a fallacy of misplaced concreteness, treating abstractions such as simple location in space and time as the concrete reality, and that the resulting scientific materialism cannot account for organisms, value or the poet's response to nature. Later chapters sketch his alternative philosophy of organism and discuss God, religion and the requisites of social progress.
