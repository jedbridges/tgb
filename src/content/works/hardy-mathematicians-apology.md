synopsis: >-
  A short essay written by a Cambridge mathematician in his sixties who felt his creative powers were gone. Hardy argues that mathematics is worth doing for its beauty and not for its uses, that pure mathematics is more real and more permanent than the applied kind, and that the best mathematics, like Euclid's proof that there are infinitely many primes, is serious because of the ideas it connects. He is candid that mathematics is a young man's game and that his own life has been justified only by having added something to knowledge.
