synopsis: >-
  Gilbert, physician to Elizabeth I, spent eighteen years experimenting with lodestones and compass needles and published his results in six books. He distinguishes magnetic attraction from the electric attraction of rubbed amber, for which he coined the word electricus, describes the poles and orientation of magnets, and uses a spherical lodestone he calls a terrella to show that the earth itself is a great magnet, which explains the compass, magnetic dip and variation. The final book argues that the earth rotates. It is often called the first modern work of experimental physics.
