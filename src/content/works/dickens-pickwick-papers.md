  Dickens's first novel, issued in monthly parts, follows the stout, innocent Samuel Pickwick and his three friends on their travels through the England of coaches and inns, with his cockney servant Sam Weller as guide. The loose comic episodes darken when Pickwick's landlady sues him for breach of promise; he refuses to pay the damages and goes to the Fleet prison, where the book's view of the law and of poverty hardens.
