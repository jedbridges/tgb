synopsis: >-
  A long review of Lockhart's Life of Scott, written for the London and Westminster Review. Carlyle uses the biography to take the measure of the most successful writer of the age and finds him healthy, honest, immensely capable and finally not great, because he wrote for money and amusement rather than out of inner necessity. He describes the Waverley novels as having taught readers that history was made by living men rather than by abstractions, praises Scott's manliness and good sense, and then tells the story of Abbotsford and the crash of 1826 as a tragedy of a man who spent his talent buying land.
