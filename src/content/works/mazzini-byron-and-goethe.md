## Overview

About twenty-five pages, an essay the Italian revolutionary Giuseppe Mazzini wrote in London exile, published in the Monthly Chronicle in 1839. Mazzini takes two major poets as the twin summits of the age just ended, Byron the poet of the individual, of subjective will and rebellion, and Goethe the poet of objective contemplation and serene detachment.

He argues that each is incomplete, that the age of individualism they represent is over, and that the poetry of the future must serve humanity and the collective progress of peoples. Writing as a practicing revolutionary rather than a detached literary critic shapes the essay's entire argument throughout.

## What happens

**Positioning Byron and Goethe as twin summits.** Mazzini opens by positioning Byron and Goethe together as the twin summits of the age of individualism just ended, treating these two major poets as complementary rather than opposed embodiments of a single completed historical and artistic era.

**Byron as the poet of subjective will and rebellion.** Mazzini characterizes Byron specifically as the poet of the individual, of subjective will and rebellion, representing one central pole of Romantic individualist achievement through his poetry's characteristic emphasis on personal defiance and assertion.

**Goethe as the poet of objective contemplation.** Mazzini characterizes Goethe as the poet of objective contemplation and serene detachment, representing the opposite pole from Byron within the same broader individualist era, a contemplative rather than rebellious mode of poetic achievement.

**Arguing each poet is incomplete.** Mazzini argues that each poet, despite genuine individual greatness, remains incomplete, Byron's subjective rebellion and Goethe's objective detachment each capturing only half of what poetry should actually accomplish rather than achieving full artistic completeness.

**Declaring the age of individualism over.** Building on this diagnosis of incompleteness, Mazzini declares that the entire age of individualism Byron and Goethe represent between them has now genuinely ended, a claim positioning both poets as summits of a completed rather than continuing historical period.

**Calling for poetry to serve collective progress.** The essay concludes by calling for a new poetry of the future that must serve humanity and the collective progress of peoples, a specifically political vision reflecting Mazzini's own considered revolutionary conviction as an actively organizing Italian nationalist.

## How to read it

Read this essay as the work of a practicing revolutionary rather than a detached literary critic; Mazzini's specific vantage point as an exiled Italian nationalist actively organizing the Young Italy movement shapes every stage of his argument about Byron, Goethe and poetry's proper future direction.

Pay close attention to how Mazzini positions Byron and Goethe as complementary rather than opposed; treating subjective rebellion and objective contemplation as two halves of a single incomplete achievement, rather than simply preferring one poet over the other, is central to his broader argument.

Notice how directly Mazzini's literary judgment fuses with explicit political program; his claim that poetry must serve collective human progress is inseparable from his own revolutionary nationalist commitments, making this essay a genuinely distinctive fusion of aesthetic and political argument.

Consider the essay's historical positioning, declaring an entire poetic era finished from within that era's own immediate aftermath, as a bold critical claim; Mazzini was writing close enough to Byron's and Goethe's actual careers that declaring their shared individualist age already over required real critical confidence.

Read this essay alongside Byron's own Manfred and Goethe's Faust to test Mazzini's characterization directly against the actual poetry he is describing; encountering both poets' work firsthand lets you evaluate whether his specific portrait of subjective rebellion against objective contemplation accurately captures their genuine achievement.
