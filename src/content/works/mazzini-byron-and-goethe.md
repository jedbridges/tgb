  An essay the Italian revolutionary wrote in London exile, published in the Monthly Chronicle in 1839. Mazzini takes the two poets as the twin summits of the age just ended, Byron the poet of the individual, of subjective will and rebellion, and Goethe the poet of objective contemplation and serene detachment. He argues that each is incomplete, that the age of individualism they represent is over, and that the poetry of the future must serve humanity and the collective progress of peoples.
