synopsis: >-
  Advice on raising a gentleman's son, expanded from letters Locke wrote to a friend. It begins with the body, prescribing cold water, thin shoes and plain food, and then turns to the mind, arguing that the point of education is virtue and self-command rather than learning, that children should be governed by esteem and disgrace rather than the rod, and that they learn best through play and example. Locke ranks Latin and rhetoric far below good character, and he recommends a trade for every gentleman.
