synopsis: >-
  A poem of 744 lines in heroic couplets, published anonymously when Pope was twenty-three, on how to judge poetry and the faults of critics. Part one argues that good criticism follows nature and the rules the ancients drew from her; part two lists the causes of bad judgement, pride, imperfect learning, love of parts rather than the whole, and fashion, with examples of sound echoing sense; part three describes the ideal critic and gives a short history of criticism from Aristotle to Boileau. It is the source of a little learning is a dangerous thing, to err is human, to forgive divine, and fools rush in where angels fear to tread.
