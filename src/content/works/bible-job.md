  A prose frame tells how God, prompted by the Adversary, permits the ruin of Job, a blameless and prosperous man, who loses his children, his wealth and his health. The long central poem is a debate between Job, who insists on his innocence and demands a hearing, and three friends who argue that suffering must be deserved. A fourth speaker, Elihu, intervenes; then God answers Job out of the whirlwind with questions about the creation that do not explain his suffering. Job submits, and the frame closes with his fortunes restored.
