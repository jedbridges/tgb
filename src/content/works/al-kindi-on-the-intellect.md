synopsis: >-
  A very short epistle by the first philosopher of the Arabic tradition, explaining the doctrine of the intellect he attributes to Aristotle. Al-Kindi distinguishes four intellects: the intellect always in act, which is the cause of all thinking; the intellect in potency in the soul; the intellect that has passed from potency to act by acquiring intelligible forms; and the intellect that can display those forms at will. It set the terms for the later Arabic and Latin debates over the active intellect.
