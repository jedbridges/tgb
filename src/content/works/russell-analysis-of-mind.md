synopsis: >-
  Fifteen lectures in which Russell tries to build an account of mind out of the same neutral stuff as matter. Drawing on American behaviourism and William James, he treats instinct, habit, desire, memory, images, belief and consciousness one by one, arguing that none of them requires a mental substance distinct from the world of physics. Mind and matter are two ways of grouping the same events, a view he calls neutral monism. The book marks his turn from logic and epistemology toward psychology and the philosophy of science.
