synopsis: >-
  Socrates questions the visiting rhetorician Gorgias about what his art is, then argues with his pupil Polus and the ambitious politician Callicles about whether rhetoric is anything more than flattery. Polus claims the tyrant is happy; Socrates replies that doing wrong is worse than suffering it, and that the wrongdoer who escapes punishment is worst off of all. Callicles defends the natural right of the strong and a life of unlimited appetite, and the dialogue ends with a myth of judgement after death.
