## Overview

About one hundred and forty pages, twelve essays James published in journals in 1904 and 1905 and planned as a book, collected in 1912 after his death by Ralph Barton Perry. The opening essay asks whether consciousness exists and answers that it is a function, not an entity.

The others develop a metaphysics of pure experience, in which mind and matter are the same stuff related in different ways, relations are as directly experienced as the things they relate, and the world is a pluralistic universe rather than a block.

## What happens

**Does consciousness exist?** James denies that consciousness is an entity or stuff, and argues that the same experience, a room for instance, is counted as physical when taken with the history of the house and as mental when taken with the history of the person, the difference lying in the relations and not in the material.

**A world of pure experience.** Radical empiricism admits into philosophy only what is experienced and excludes nothing that is, and since relations are experienced, the connective tissue of the world is given directly and no transcendental glue is needed.

**The thing and its relations.** James answers the objection that the same experience cannot be in two places by showing that a thing's relations to other things are what it is in each context, and that the paradox is only verbal.

**How two minds can know one thing.** He argues that two minds share an object when their experiences terminate in the same experience, and that the common world is built up from such terminations rather than assumed beneath them.

**Activity and the experience of it.** The feeling of effort and the experience of causation are real parts of experience and not illusions, and the question of whether we are really active is one that experience itself has to answer.

**Pluralism.** The closing essays defend a universe that is many and growing, held together by the connections experience supplies and never completed, against the monism of the absolute idealists, and prepare the ground for A Pluralistic Universe.

## How to read it

Read the opening essay's denial of consciousness as a denial of a stuff rather than of experience, and notice that James is trying to keep everything we feel while dropping the entity that was supposed to feel it.

Pay close attention to the claim that relations are experienced, and to how it answers Hume from within empiricism rather than from outside it.

Notice the example of the room, in the house and in the biography at once, and use it to understand what pure experience means before the term becomes abstract.

Consider the pluralistic universe of the closing essays, and ask what the metaphysics gives to the pragmatism and the ethics of the will to believe.

Read these essays alongside Russell's Analysis of Mind to see the neutral monism James proposed taken up by a philosopher of a very different temperament, and what changed in the taking.

## Questions it raises

- If consciousness is a function and not an entity, what is it that performs the function, and has James answered the question or renamed it?
- Can the same experience really be both mental and physical depending on its relations, and does the example of the room prove it or only make it vivid?
- Are relations directly experienced, as James claims against Hume, and how would one show that the feeling of because is not an inference?
- What does a pluralistic universe, held together piecemeal and never finished, do to the idea of truth, and does it leave room for the truth pragmatism needs?
- Why did James leave these essays uncollected, and does the book Perry made from them say what James would have said?
- What did the neutral monism James proposed become in Russell and later philosophy of mind, and does the current interest in it vindicate him?
