  An address Huxley gave at the opening of Josiah Mason's Science College in Birmingham in 1880, published the next year in Science and Culture, and Other Essays. Answering those who held that only a classical education could produce culture, he argues that a training in the methods and results of physical science does so just as well, that the Renaissance humanists were themselves rebels against a dead learning, and that a man who knows no science is as poorly educated as one who knows no literature.
