synopsis: >-
  Foucault opens with the public execution of the regicide Damiens in 1757 and a prison timetable from eighty years later, and asks how punishment changed from spectacle on the body to discipline of the soul. He traces the reformers' proposals, the rise of the timetable, the drill and the examination in schools, hospitals, barracks and factories, and takes Bentham's Panopticon as the model of a power that works by making people visible. The prison, he argues, was born from these disciplines and, though it fails to reform, succeeds in producing delinquency that can be managed.
