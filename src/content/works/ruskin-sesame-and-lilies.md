synopsis: >-
  Two lectures given in Manchester in 1864 and published the next year, with a third added in 1871. Of Kings' Treasuries argues that books are the company of the best minds and that the English do not read, valuing wealth and advancement over education. Of Queens' Gardens describes the separate powers of men and women, assigning women the guardianship of the home and of moral order, and calls for girls to be educated to fit them for it. Enormously popular as a school prize, it became a target for later feminist criticism.
