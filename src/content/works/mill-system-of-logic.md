synopsis: >-
  Mill's longest book, in six parts. He begins with names and propositions, argues that all real inference is from particulars to particulars and that the syllogism registers rather than produces knowledge, and then builds his central account of induction: the uniformity of nature as the ground of all inference, and the four experimental methods of agreement, difference, residues and concomitant variations by which causes are found. Later books treat fallacies, and the last proposes a logic of the moral sciences, with its own account of how social laws relate to laws of individual human nature.
