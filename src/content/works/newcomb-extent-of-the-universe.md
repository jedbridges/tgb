  A popular essay by the leading American astronomer of his day, first published in a magazine in 1902 and collected in Side-Lights on Astronomy. Newcomb asks how large the universe is and whether it is finite. He explains how the distances of stars are measured by parallax and by their motions, argues from the counts of faint stars that the stellar system is bounded rather than infinite, and reasons that if the stars extended without limit the night sky would be uniformly bright.
