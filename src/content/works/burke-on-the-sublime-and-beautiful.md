synopsis: >-
  Burke's early treatise on aesthetics, written in his twenties, separates two feelings that earlier writers had run together. The sublime arises from whatever excites the idea of pain and danger without actually threatening us, obscurity, vastness, power, darkness and infinity, and is the strongest emotion the mind can feel. The beautiful arises from smallness, smoothness, gradual variation and delicacy and inspires love. Burke grounds both in the body's nerves, and closes with a section on how words move us more than pictures do.
