synopsis: >-
  Comte's six-volume course, delivered as lectures and published between 1830 and 1842, announces a philosophy that confines knowledge to observable facts and the laws connecting them, and refuses questions about first causes and final ends. He states a law of three stages through which every branch of thought passes: theological, metaphysical and positive. He then arranges the sciences in a hierarchy of increasing complexity and decreasing generality, from mathematics through astronomy, physics, chemistry and biology, and adds a new one at the top, social physics or sociology, whose business is to find the laws of society and so put politics on a scientific footing.
