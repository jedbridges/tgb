synopsis: >-
  The two letters that between them summarise Epicurus' system, both preserved in Diogenes Laertius. The Letter to Herodotus is a compressed outline of his physics: everything is atoms and void, the atoms are infinite and always in motion, worlds are countless, the soul is material and perishes with the body, and the senses are the criterion of truth. The Letter to Menoeceus draws the ethical conclusions: the gods do not interfere, death is nothing to us, and a pleasant life is a prudent one.
