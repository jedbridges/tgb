  Douglass's account of his first twenty-seven years, written to prove that a fugitive slave could describe slavery himself. He was born on the eastern shore of Maryland, separated from his mother, and saw an aunt whipped before he was old enough to understand it. Sent to Baltimore, he learned the alphabet from his master's wife until she was forbidden to teach him, then went on secretly with scraps of newspaper and the help of white boys in the street. Hired out to the slave-breaker Covey, he fought him and won. In 1838 he escaped to New York and became an abolitionist lecturer.
