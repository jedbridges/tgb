synopsis: >-
  Based on Russell's William James Lectures at Harvard, the book asks what it means for a sentence to be true and how language connects to experience. It works upward from object-words learned by direct confrontation with things, through sentences describing experience, to logical words, egocentric particulars such as this and now, and the basic propositions on which empirical knowledge rests. Russell defends a correspondence theory of truth against the verificationism of the logical positivists, and argues that truth must be allowed to outrun what we can know.
