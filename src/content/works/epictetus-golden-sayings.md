synopsis: >-
  Not a work Epictetus composed but a Victorian anthology, Hastings Crossley's selection of the most quotable passages from the Discourses and the Handbook, printed in the Harvard Classics beside Marcus Aurelius and Plato. The arrangement is thematic rather than by book, so the same doctrine appears repeatedly in different words: what is in our power, the uselessness of complaint, the actor who did not choose his part, the value of an obstacle. It is a good first taste of Stoicism and a poor substitute for reading a book of the Discourses through.
