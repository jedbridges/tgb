## Overview

About sixty pages, three papers by Louis Pasteur, written between 1878 and 1880 and gathered as selected for the Harvard Classics. The Physiological Theory of Fermentation, from his 1876 studies of beer, shows that fermentation is the work of living micro-organisms rather than a purely chemical process.

The Germ Theory and Its Applications to Medicine and Surgery, read to the Academy of Medicine in 1878, connects these microbes to disease and to Lister's antiseptic surgery, and Extension of the Germ Theory in 1880 applies it to further diseases, including chicken cholera and the first attenuated vaccine.

## What happens

**Fermentation as living process.** The Physiological Theory of Fermentation, drawing on Pasteur's 1876 studies of beer, demonstrates that fermentation results from the activity of living micro-organisms rather than a purely chemical process as previously believed.

**Overturning chemical theory.** This finding directly overturns considerable established chemical theory, requiring biology's living processes where chemistry alone had seemed sufficient explanation for a phenomenon long studied primarily through chemical analysis.

**Connecting microbes to disease.** The Germ Theory and Its Applications to Medicine and Surgery, read to the Academy of Medicine in 1878, extends the fermentation findings by connecting these same microbes directly to disease itself.

**Explaining Lister's antiseptic success.** This same paper connects germ theory to Lister's antiseptic surgery, giving his already successful practical methods a genuine theoretical foundation explaining exactly why his techniques actually worked to prevent infection.

**Extending germ theory to chicken cholera.** Extension of the Germ Theory, published in 1880, applies the theory to further diseases including chicken cholera, demonstrating the framework's explanatory power beyond the human diseases that had originally motivated Pasteur's research.

**The first attenuated vaccine.** This same extension paper introduces the first attenuated vaccine, a genuinely novel preventive technique building directly on germ theory's understanding of how weakened pathogens could confer immunity against fuller disease.

## How to read it

Read the Physiological Theory of Fermentation's demonstration that fermentation is the work of living micro-organisms as overturning a considerable body of established chemical theory, requiring biology's living processes where chemistry alone had seemed sufficient explanation.

Pay close attention to how the Germ Theory paper's direct connection of microbes to disease gave Lister's already successful antiseptic surgery a genuine theoretical foundation, explaining exactly why his practical methods actually worked rather than merely that they did.

Notice how Extension of the Germ Theory's application to chicken cholera and the first attenuated vaccine demonstrated germ theory's explanatory power extended well beyond the human diseases that had originally motivated Pasteur's research, opening an entire field of application.

Consider reading these three papers together as tracing germ theory's rapid development across just four years, from specific fermentation studies through general medical application to genuinely novel preventive technique, one of medicine's fastest and most consequential theoretical transformations.

Read these papers alongside Lister's own Antiseptic Principle to see directly how Pasteur's theoretical breakthrough connects to and explains the practical surgical innovation Lister had already developed independently before Pasteur's germ theory gave it full scientific grounding.

- How does the connection Pasteur draws between fermentation microbes and disease-causing germs shape your understanding of how scientific concepts transfer across seemingly distinct domains? Does this transfer represent genuine insight or a looser analogy later research would need to refine?
- What does giving Lister's antiseptic surgery a theoretical foundation after the fact suggest about the relationship between successful practice and scientific understanding? Can effective methods precede genuine explanation, and does subsequent theory change how such methods should be applied?
- How significant is the first attenuated vaccine's introduction in Extension of the Germ Theory, given how central vaccination has become to subsequent medicine? Does this brief mention adequately convey the technique's eventual transformative importance?
- Given that these three papers span only four years, what does this rapid progression from fermentation studies to general medical application to preventive technique suggest about the pace of scientific advance once a genuinely productive theoretical framework has been established?
- What does the application of germ theory to chicken cholera, a veterinary rather than human disease, suggest about how Pasteur understood the theory's proper scope? Does this extension reveal confidence in germ theory's genuinely general applicability across different forms of life?
