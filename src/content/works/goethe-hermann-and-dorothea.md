## Overview

About ninety pages, a short epic poem by Johann Wolfgang von Goethe, composed in nine cantos of classical hexameter, each canto named for one of the nine Muses, published in 1797 at a moment when the French Revolutionary Wars were producing genuine, immediate disruption across German-speaking Europe rather than remaining a distant historical concern. Goethe deliberately deploys epic's traditionally grand ancient form, the hexameter line historically associated with Homer and Virgil, to narrate a story genuinely modest in scale, a prosperous small-town innkeeper's son falling in love with a refugee he encounters while distributing food and clothing to displaced families fleeing the war's advancing chaos.

This formal choice, epic dignity applied to domestic, private subject matter, functions as the poem's own implicit argument: ordinary courage, ordinary love and ordinary family conflict, Goethe suggests through his chosen form, deserve the same serious literary attention traditionally reserved for battlefield heroism and grand historical events, even, or perhaps especially, at a moment when vast historical forces were reshaping the world immediately beyond the poem's quiet Rhine town setting.

## What happens

**Refugees pass through the town.** The poem opens with families displaced by the advancing French revolutionary armies passing through a small German town on the Rhine, prompting the town's more prosperous residents, including Hermann's family, to organize assistance for the refugees' immediate material needs.

**Hermann meets Dorothea.** Hermann, the son of the town's prosperous innkeeper, brings a cart of provisions and clothing out to the refugees and there notices Dorothea, a young woman managing and caring for the displaced families around her with a capability and dignity that immediately impresses him, an introduction that establishes her character through demonstrated competence rather than through conventional physical or romantic description.

**The father's objection.** Returning home genuinely smitten, Hermann faces his father's clear preference for a wealthier, more socially advantageous match than a penniless refugee could provide, a paternal objection that sets up the poem's central family tension between parental expectation and Hermann's own developing romantic conviction.

**Investigation among the refugees.** Hermann's mother, considerably more sympathetic to her son's actual feelings than his father, arranges for the town pastor and the local apothecary to travel out among the refugees and make careful, discreet inquiries into Dorothea's actual character, family background and reputation, a practical, socially appropriate method of investigation that will ultimately vindicate Hermann's own romantic judgment.

**The misunderstanding.** When Dorothea agrees to return to the town with Hermann, she understands the arrangement to be taking a position as a household servant rather than as a prospective bride, a misunderstanding Hermann himself allows to persist for a time out of his own shyness and uncertainty about how to properly declare his actual intentions toward her.

**Resolution in betrothal.** As the accumulated evidence from the pastor's and apothecary's inquiries confirms Dorothea's genuine worth and good character, and as the underlying misunderstanding about her actual role finally becomes clear, the poem resolves into an open, mutually acknowledged betrothal between Hermann and Dorothea, a modest, private domestic resolution achieved against the backdrop of considerably larger historical upheaval the poem keeps consistently at its margins rather than at its center.

## How to read it

Read the poem's deliberate use of classical hexameter for genuinely modest domestic subject matter as its own implicit argument about what deserves serious literary and epic attention, and consider how this formal choice shapes your own sense of the story's actual stakes and significance relative to a more conventionally modest prose treatment of the same material.

Notice how Dorothea's character is established almost entirely through her demonstrated competence managing the refugee crisis rather than through conventional romantic or physical description, a characterization method that makes her practical capability, rather than simple beauty or charm, the actual basis for Hermann's attraction and the poem's broader endorsement of the match.

Pay attention to how the poem keeps the French Revolutionary Wars constantly present at its margins, the actual cause of the refugee crisis that brings Hermann and Dorothea together, while resolutely focusing its main narrative attention on private, domestic resolution rather than directly narrating the war's larger military and political events, a deliberate structural choice worth considering directly.

Consider the investigation the pastor and apothecary conduct among the refugees as itself a meaningful narrative device, a socially appropriate, practical method for resolving the father's legitimate concern about Dorothea's actual background without requiring direct confrontation between Hermann and his father.

Read this poem alongside Goethe's own autobiography, Poetry and Truth, to see how directly his personal experience of disrupted courtship and family expectation, themes recurring throughout his own life, inform even a poem explicitly set within a fictional small town rather than drawing directly on his own biography.

## Questions it raises

- What does establishing Dorothea's character primarily through her demonstrated practical competence, rather than through conventional romantic description, suggest about what the poem actually values in a potential marriage partner, compared to more conventional epic or romantic treatments of courtship?
- How does keeping the French Revolutionary Wars constantly present at the poem's margins, without directly narrating its larger events, shape the poem's actual relationship to the genuine historical crisis it depends on for its central situation? Does this structural choice feel like a deliberate artistic statement or an evasion of the war's actual seriousness?
- What does the resolution of Hermann and Dorothea's misunderstanding through practical investigation, rather than through direct emotional confrontation between Hermann and his father, suggest about how the poem understands the proper relationship between individual romantic feeling and family and social obligation?
- Given that Goethe wrote and published this poem during a period of genuine, immediate historical crisis for German-speaking Europe, what does the poem's choice to focus on private domestic resolution rather than direct political or military engagement suggest about literature's proper relationship to contemporary historical upheaval?
- How does this poem's treatment of displaced refugees, presented with genuine sympathy but ultimately serving as the occasion for a domestic love story, compare to how later literature has treated similar situations of displacement and crisis? Does centering Hermann and Dorothea's romance risk subordinating the refugees' own genuine suffering to a comparatively minor domestic concern?
