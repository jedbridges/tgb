synopsis: >-
  The speech Carlyle gave in April 1866 on being installed as Rector of the University of Edinburgh, more than fifty years after he had been a poor student there. Speaking without notes, he tells the students that the true university is a collection of books and that the real work of education is what they do on their own, by reading well and steadily. He praises industry, silence and honesty, warns against showy talk and against reading merely to be thought clever, urges attention to history and to the mathematics and languages that discipline the mind, and closes on the duty of doing whatever work is given to you thoroughly.
