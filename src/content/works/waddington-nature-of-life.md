synopsis: >-
  A short book of lectures by a British embryologist and geneticist on what biology had learned by 1960 and what it still could not explain. Waddington sets out the new molecular account of the gene and of heredity, then argues that it is not enough on its own: what needs explaining is development, the way a fertilised egg builds an organism, and the way an organism as a whole meets its environment. He introduces his own ideas of the epigenetic landscape and canalisation, discusses evolution as a system with several feedback loops rather than one, and closes on mind, human society and the place of values in a scientific picture of life.
