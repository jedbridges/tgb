synopsis: >-
  Kant's short foundational work in ethics sets out to find the supreme principle of morality. Starting from the claim that nothing is good without qualification except a good will, he argues that moral worth lies in acting from duty rather than inclination, and that duty is obedience to a law reason gives itself. He formulates this law as the categorical imperative, in several versions, including the demand to act only on maxims one could will to be universal laws and to treat humanity always as an end and never merely as a means, and closes by arguing that morality presupposes freedom.
