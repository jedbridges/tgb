synopsis: >-
  Leibniz's chapter-by-chapter reply to Locke's Essay Concerning Human Understanding, written as a dialogue between Philalethes, who speaks for Locke, and Theophilus, who speaks for Leibniz. Against the mind as a blank slate, Leibniz argues for innate ideas and truths that experience only awakens, like veins in a block of marble. He discusses unconscious petites perceptions, personal identity, language, and the limits of knowledge. Locke died in 1704 and Leibniz withheld the book; it appeared in 1765.
