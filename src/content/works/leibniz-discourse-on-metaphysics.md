synopsis: >-
  A treatise in thirty-seven sections that Leibniz wrote in 1686 and summarised for the theologian Arnauld, unpublished in his lifetime. It argues that God, being perfect, has created the best of all possible worlds, and that every individual substance contains in its complete concept everything that will ever happen to it, so that each substance mirrors the whole universe. Leibniz reconciles this with human freedom, criticises Descartes on force and motion, and closes with the soul's union with God.
