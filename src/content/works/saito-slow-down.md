## Overview

About two hundred and ninety pages, a Japanese bestseller of 2020 by the young philosopher Kohei Saito, translated into English in 2024. Saito argues that endless economic growth is incompatible with a habitable climate, that green growth and technological fixes are illusions, and that the late Marx, in his notebooks on ecology and pre-capitalist communities, points toward the answer.

He proposes degrowth communism, a democratically planned economy of shared commons, shorter working hours and production for use rather than for profit.

## What happens

**Growth incompatible with climate.** Saito opens by arguing that endless economic growth is fundamentally incompatible with maintaining a habitable climate, rejecting any framework that treats continued expansion as compatible with genuine environmental sustainability.

**Rejecting green growth.** Saito argues that green growth and technological fixes are illusions, directly challenging the mainstream environmental policy consensus that decoupling economic growth from emissions offers a viable path forward.

**Turning to the late Marx.** Saito turns to the late Marx, examining notebooks on ecology and pre-capitalist communities that Saito argues point toward the answer, recovering understudied material within Marx's considerable broader corpus.

**Ecology in Marx's late thought.** Saito argues Marx himself moved toward genuinely ecological thinking in his final years, thought scholars have too easily overlooked in favor of his more familiar political-economic writings on capital and labor.

**Proposing degrowth communism.** Saito proposes degrowth communism specifically, a democratically planned economy built around shared commons rather than private accumulation or centralized state control alone.

**Shorter hours and production for use.** This proposed system features shorter working hours and production organized for genuine use rather than for profit, offering a specific programmatic alternative rather than only diagnostic critique of growth-oriented economics.

## How to read it

Read Saito's rejection of green growth and technological fixes as illusions as directly challenging the mainstream environmental policy consensus that decoupling economic growth from emissions represents climate change's most politically viable solution.

Pay close attention to how the turn to the late Marx's notebooks on ecology and pre-capitalist communities recovers genuinely understudied material, using this scholarship to argue Marx himself moved toward ecological thinking too easily overlooked by subsequent readers.

Notice how the proposal for degrowth communism, a democratically planned economy of shared commons, shorter working hours and production for use, offers a specific programmatic alternative rather than only critique of growth-oriented economic organization.

Consider the book's remarkable commercial success as a Japanese bestseller before its English translation, demonstrating genuine popular appetite for radical economic alternatives to growth-oriented climate policy well beyond typical academic ecological Marxism's usual audience.

Read this book alongside Pollin's own De-Growth vs. a Green New Deal to see two sharply opposed positions within contemporary climate economics, one defending continued growth through massive clean investment, the other rejecting growth as fundamentally incompatible with genuine sustainability.
