synopsis: >-
  A handbook of number theory in two books by a Neopythagorean of the late first century, written in Gerasa in Roman Syria. Book I classifies numbers as even and odd, prime and composite, perfect, abundant and deficient, and Book II treats figurate numbers, such as triangular and square, and the arithmetic, geometric and harmonic proportions. Without proofs, it presents number as the pattern of the cosmos, and through Boethius's Latin version it was the standard arithmetic textbook of the Middle Ages.
