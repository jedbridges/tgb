synopsis: >-
  A twelve-volume comparative history of some twenty-one civilisations, from the Egyptian and Sumerian to the Western, published over twenty-seven years. Toynbee argues that civilisations, not nations, are the units of history, that they arise through creative responses to challenges, grow through the leadership of creative minorities, and break down when those minorities become merely dominant and the masses secede. The later volumes give the universal churches that emerge from dying civilisations the central role.
