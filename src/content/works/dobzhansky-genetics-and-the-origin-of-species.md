  The book that brought Mendelian genetics and Darwinian natural selection together and started the modern synthesis. Based on Dobzhansky's 1936 Jesup Lectures at Columbia, it treats mutation as the raw material of evolution, describes variation in natural populations of Drosophila, and shows how selection, genetic drift, and isolating mechanisms such as hybrid sterility turn variation within a species into differences between species.
