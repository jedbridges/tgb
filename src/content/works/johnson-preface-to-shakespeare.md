synopsis: >-
  Johnson's preface to his 1765 edition of Shakespeare's plays argues that Shakespeare has stood the test of time because he is the poet of general nature, whose characters are species rather than individuals. Johnson lists Shakespeare's faults frankly, his carelessness with plots, his puns, his failure to teach morality, and then dismantles the neoclassical rule of the unities of time and place, observing that spectators always know they are in a theatre. He also defends mixing comedy and tragedy and describes the textual problems he faced as an editor.
