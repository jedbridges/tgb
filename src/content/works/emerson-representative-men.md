synopsis: >-
  Seven lectures, first delivered in 1845 and printed in 1850, on the uses of great men. An opening essay argues that we need great men not to worship but to use, because each one shows a capacity that is latent in everybody. Six portraits follow: Plato the philosopher, Swedenborg the mystic, Montaigne the sceptic, Shakespeare the poet, Napoleon the man of the world and Goethe the writer. Each is praised for the power he embodies and then measured against his limits, Napoleon most severely of all.
