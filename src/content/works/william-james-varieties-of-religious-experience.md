  James's Gifford Lectures at Edinburgh in 1901 and 1902, which study religion not as doctrine or institution but as the feelings and experiences of individuals. Drawing on hundreds of first-person accounts, he describes the religion of healthy-mindedness and of the sick soul, the divided self and conversion, saintliness and its value, and mysticism. He concludes that religious experience is real and consequential whatever its ultimate cause, and offers his own over-belief in a wider self.
