synopsis: >-
  A short and partly fragmentary treatise on poetry as imitation, concentrating on tragedy. Aristotle defines tragedy as the imitation of a serious and complete action that arouses pity and fear, ranks plot above character, and describes reversal, recognition and the tragic flaw, using Sophocles' Oedipus as the model. Later chapters treat diction and compare tragedy with epic; the book on comedy is lost.
