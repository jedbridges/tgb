synopsis: >-
  A confession of faith by a young Norwich physician, written for himself around 1635, printed without his consent in 1642 and authorised in 1643. In the first part Browne describes a religion that reconciles the new science with the Church of England, delights in mysteries, and loves to lose himself in an O altitudo. In the second he treats charity and his own temperament. The book is prized for its slow, Latinate prose and its tolerant curiosity about everything.
