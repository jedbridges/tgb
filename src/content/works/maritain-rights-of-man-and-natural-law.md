  A wartime essay, written in New York in 1942, that grounds human rights in the natural law tradition. The first part distinguishes the human person from the individual and argues that society exists for the person and the person is ordered to the common good. The second part derives the rights of the person, the civic person and the working person from natural law. The book influenced the drafting of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, on which Maritain worked for UNESCO.
