synopsis: >-
  The fullest surviving account of ancient Pyrrhonian scepticism, in three books. The first explains what scepticism is, the ability to set appearances and arguments against one another so that the mind suspends judgement and tranquillity follows, and lays out the ten modes of Aenesidemus and the five modes of Agrippa for producing that suspension. Books two and three apply the method to the logic, physics and ethics of the dogmatic schools, arguing against criteria of truth, proof, causes, gods and the existence of anything good or bad by nature.
