synopsis: >-
  An analysis of the rich as an anthropologist might study a tribe. Veblen argues that the leisure class arose when predatory exploit became more honourable than productive work, and that its members prove their standing by conspicuous leisure and conspicuous consumption, wasting time and goods in ways that visibly cost money. He applies the idea to dress, manners, sport, religion, higher learning and the position of women, whose idleness and finery display their husbands' wealth. The deadpan style is part of the argument.
