synopsis: >-
  Cicero's last philosophical work, written in the autumn of 44 BCE after Caesar's assassination and addressed to his son Marcus, then a student in Athens. Drawing on the Stoic Panaetius, Book I treats what is honourable, dividing virtue into wisdom, justice, greatness of spirit and decorum; Book II treats what is useful, including how to win goodwill and give generously; Book III argues that the honourable and the useful never really conflict, with cases such as the grain merchant at Rhodes and Regulus keeping his oath to Carthage. The programs that list Cicero's Works generally centre on this text with the orations and dialogues on friendship and old age.
