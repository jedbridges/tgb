synopsis: >-
  Lectures given in Dublin in 1943 by the Austrian physicist, asking how the events inside a living organism can be accounted for by physics and chemistry. Schrödinger argues that the gene must be a stable molecule, an aperiodic crystal carrying a code-script in the arrangement of its atoms, and that mutations are quantum jumps. He explains how life maintains order by feeding on negative entropy from its surroundings, and closes with an epilogue on determinism and free will. Watson and Crick both said the book turned them toward the problem of the gene.
