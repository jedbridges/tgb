synopsis: >-
  A short handbook of Stoic practice, compiled by Epictetus's student Arrian out of the longer Discourses. It opens with the division on which everything else rests: some things are up to us, namely judgement, desire and aversion, and some things are not, namely body, reputation, office and everything external. Misery comes from wanting what is not ours to control. The fifty-three brief chapters then apply this to grief, illness, insults, family, dinner parties, public office and death, and end with the reminder that a person is judged by what they do with the role they are given.
