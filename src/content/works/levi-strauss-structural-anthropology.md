synopsis: >-
  Seventeen essays from 1944 to 1957 in which Lévi-Strauss proposes that kinship, myth, ritual and art can be analysed like language, as systems of relations among elements rather than as collections of things. Drawing on the structural linguistics of Jakobson and Saussure, he treats marriage rules as exchanges of women, myths as sets of transformations, and the shaman's cure as a kind of psychoanalysis. Includes The Structural Study of Myth, Structural Analysis in Linguistics and in Anthropology, and the essays on the sorcerer and on split representation.
