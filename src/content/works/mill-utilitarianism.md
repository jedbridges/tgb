synopsis: >-
  Five essays, first serialized in Fraser's Magazine, defending the greatest happiness principle: actions are right in proportion as they promote pleasure and the absence of pain. Mill answers the charge that this is a doctrine fit for swine by distinguishing higher from lower pleasures and appealing to the judgement of those who have known both. He explains what sanctions bind us to the principle, offers his notorious proof that happiness is the only thing desired for its own sake, and devotes the last and longest chapter to showing that justice can be accounted for in utilitarian terms.
