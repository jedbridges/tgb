## Overview

About a hundred pages, the first major German comedy, written by Gotthold Ephraim Lessing and set in a Berlin inn just after the Seven Years' War, published in 1767. Major von Tellheim, discharged and under suspicion of dishonesty despite his actual innocence, has broken off his engagement to the Saxon heiress Minna because he will not bring a ruined name into marriage, treating his compromised reputation as a genuine obstacle rather than something love could simply overcome.

Minna arrives in search of him, and when arguments fail, tricks him by pretending she too has been disinherited, so that his honour now obliges him to marry her. A letter from the king clears him, and Minna teases him a while longer before the happy ending.

## What happens

**Tellheim's broken engagement.** The play opens with Major von Tellheim's already broken engagement to Minna von Barnhelm, discharged from military service and under suspicion of dishonesty despite his actual innocence, having ended the relationship specifically because he refuses to bring a compromised reputation into marriage.

**Minna's arrival in Berlin.** Minna arrives in Berlin actively searching for Tellheim, determined to resolve their broken engagement despite his own stated refusal, setting up the play's central comic and romantic conflict between his stubborn principle and her equally determined pursuit.

**Failed direct argument.** Minna's initial direct attempts to argue Tellheim out of his position fail entirely, his sense of honor proving too rigid to yield to sentiment or straightforward persuasion alone, requiring her to devise a more indirect strategic approach.

**Minna's trick.** Minna tricks Tellheim by pretending that she too has been disinherited, restoring symmetry to their respective circumstances so that his own sense of honor now obliges him to marry her rather than continuing to refuse, using his rigid principle against itself.

**The king's letter.** A letter from the king arrives clearing Tellheim's name of the suspicion that had prompted his original broken engagement, official vindication that arrives only after Minna's own trick has already accomplished the play's actual emotional and romantic resolution.

**Minna's continued teasing.** Even after the truth comes out and Tellheim's name is cleared, Minna continues teasing him a while longer before allowing the happy ending to fully arrive, extending her own agency past the plot's formal resolution rather than simply accepting immediate reconciliation.

## How to read it

Read Tellheim's refusal to marry Minna while under suspicion as genuine psychological seriousness within an otherwise comic frame; the play takes military honor's actual weight seriously even while building toward comic resolution, giving the romantic conflict real substance beyond mere plot obstacle.

Pay close attention to how Minna's trick works specifically by using Tellheim's own rigid sense of honor against itself rather than asking him to abandon his principle entirely; this strategic cleverness, restoring symmetry rather than demanding capitulation, is the play's genuine comic and romantic achievement.

Notice how the king's letter clearing Tellheim's name arrives almost anticlimactically after Minna's trick has already resolved the central conflict; the play deliberately subordinates external, official vindication to the more genuine psychological and romantic resolution Minna's own cleverness accomplished first.

Consider Minna's continued teasing after the truth comes out as significant rather than mere comic filler; this extension of her agency past the plot's formal resolution refuses to let the happy ending arrive as simple capitulation to a man who needed considerably more than official clearance to overcome his own stubborn pride.

Read this play alongside Molière's Le Misanthrope to compare how two major European comic playwrights, working in different national traditions within a similar broader period, each built comedy around a rigid, principled male character whose stubbornness the plot must work around rather than simply overcome directly.
