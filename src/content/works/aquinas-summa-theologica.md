  Aquinas' unfinished summary of Christian theology, written as a textbook for beginners and organised in three parts of several thousand articles, each posing a question, listing objections and answering them. The First Part treats God, creation, angels and human nature; the Second treats human action, the virtues and law, including the famous Treatise on Law; the Third treats Christ and the sacraments. It draws on Aristotle, Augustine and Scripture in equal measure and is the central text of scholastic philosophy.
