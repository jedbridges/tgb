  Darwin's argument that all species descend with modification from common ancestors, and that the main cause of change is natural selection: individuals vary, more are born than can survive, and those with useful variations leave more offspring. He begins with variation under domestication, moves to the struggle for existence and natural selection, then spends the second half meeting objections from the imperfection of the fossil record, the difficulty of explaining instinct and complex organs, and the geographical distribution of living things.
