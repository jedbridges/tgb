synopsis: >-
  A short essay from Four Dissertations on a problem Hume sets himself: beauty is no quality in things but a sentiment in the mind, which seems to make all tastes equal, yet no one really thinks Ogilby as good a poet as Milton. Hume answers that although sentiment cannot be argued with directly, some sentiments are the product of better instruments. He describes the qualifications of a true judge, which are delicacy of imagination, practice, comparison, freedom from prejudice and good sense, and takes the joint verdict of such critics across time as the standard, allowing for the unavoidable differences of temperament and of age.
