synopsis: >-
  Dewey's most ambitious and most difficult book, the Carus Lectures expanded. He argues that philosophy has gone wrong by treating the objects of refined reflection as more real than the ordinary experience they came from, and proposes instead an empirical method that always traces ideas back to the situations that produced them. Nature is precarious and stable at once; mind, meaning and language emerge from organisms interacting with it; ends and values are things that arise within nature rather than above it. The last chapters treat existence as art and the place of criticism.
