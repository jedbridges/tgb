synopsis: >-
  An essay first published in the Revue des Deux Mondes in 1854, in which Renan, himself a Breton, describes the character of the Celtic peoples as timid, melancholy, imaginative, devoted to a lost cause and to the ideal woman. He surveys the Mabinogion, the Arthurian romances, the legends of the saints and the Breton folk poetry collected by La Villemarqué, and argues that the Celts gave European literature its romance and its feeling for nature. Matthew Arnold's lectures on Celtic literature took the essay as their starting point.
