synopsis: >-
  Hume's first and most ambitious book, an attempt to apply experimental method to the mind. Book I, Of the Understanding, derives all ideas from impressions and then dismantles our confidence in causation, external bodies and the self, which Hume describes as a bundle of perceptions with no observable owner. Book II, Of the Passions, explains pride, humility, love and hatred by association, and argues that reason alone can never move us to act. Book III, Of Morals, argues that moral distinctions are felt rather than deduced, that no ought follows from an is, and that justice is an artificial virtue built on convention.
