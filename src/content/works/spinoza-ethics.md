synopsis: >-
  Published after Spinoza's death and set out like Euclid, in definitions, axioms, propositions and demonstrations. Part I argues that there is only one substance, God or Nature, of which everything else is a mode, and that nothing in it is contingent or purposive. Part II treats the human mind as an idea of the body and distinguishes three kinds of knowledge. Parts III and IV analyse the emotions as effects of the one striving Spinoza calls conatus, and argue that we are in bondage as long as we are driven by passions we do not understand. Part V describes the freedom that comes from understanding, and the intellectual love of God.
