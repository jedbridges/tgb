synopsis: >-
  A short Chinese classic of eighty-one brief chapters in rhythmic, often paradoxical verse, traditionally attributed to Laozi, an older contemporary of Confucius, and probably compiled in the fourth century BCE. It teaches the Tao, the way that underlies and cannot be named, and the virtue of following it through non-action, softness, lowliness and emptiness, like water that wears down stone. Addressed partly to rulers, it advises governing so lightly that the people hardly know they are ruled.
