synopsis: >-
  Mill asks what form of government is best and answers that it is representative government, because it both protects people's interests and improves their character by giving them something to do. He then works through the machinery: the proper business of an elected assembly, which is to watch and criticise rather than to administer; the case for extending the suffrage, including to women; his arguments for plural voting weighted by education and against the secret ballot; Hare's scheme of proportional representation; the relation of a second chamber and a permanent civil service to the elected house; federalism; and the government of dependencies.
