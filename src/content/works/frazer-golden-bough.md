synopsis: >-
  Frazer begins with a rule of the sanctuary of Diana at Nemi, where the priest was a runaway slave who held office until killed by his successor, and sets out to explain it by comparing customs from every recorded society. The explanation grows into a theory of human thought passing from magic through religion to science, with chapters on sympathetic magic, taboo, sacred kings put to death when their strength fails, dying and reviving gods such as Adonis, Attis and Osiris, the corn spirit, scapegoats and fire festivals. Two volumes in 1890 grew to twelve by 1915; the 1922 abridgement is what most readers know.
