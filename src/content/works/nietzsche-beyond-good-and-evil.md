  Nine chapters of numbered aphorisms and short essays attacking the assumptions of past philosophers, who Nietzsche says dressed their prejudices up as truths. He argues that the will to truth itself needs questioning, that morality has been a device of the herd against the exceptional individual, and that a coming generation of free spirits and philosophers of the future must create values rather than discover them. The final chapters distinguish master morality from slave morality and ask what is noble.
