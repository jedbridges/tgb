synopsis: >-
  Four lectures and essays from the late 1920s, with an introductory survey, in which Bohr sets out what quantum mechanics means for the idea of describing nature at all. Because any measurement disturbs what it measures, the classical concepts of position and momentum, or of particle and wave, cannot be applied at once, and Bohr proposes complementarity: mutually exclusive descriptions that are each necessary and together exhaust what can be said. He extends the idea speculatively to biology and psychology. Programs often pair it with Discussion with Einstein on Epistemological Problems in Atomic Physics, which records his long argument with Einstein.
