  Arendt's first major book traces the conditions that made Nazi Germany and Stalin's Russia possible. Part one follows the rise of modern antisemitism and the Dreyfus affair. Part two treats imperialism, the alliance of capital and mob, race thinking, and the collapse of the nation state that left millions stateless and without the right to have rights. Part three describes totalitarianism as a new form of government that uses mass movements, ideology and terror to make human beings superfluous, with the concentration camp as its central institution.
