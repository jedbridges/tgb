  One hundred and seven essays in three books, written and rewritten in the tower of Montaigne's estate between 1572 and his death. They begin as commonplace-book reflections on the ancients and grow into a portrait of one man thinking: on friendship, on cannibals, on cruelty, on the education of children, on the inconsistency of our actions, on repentance, on experience. The longest, the Apology for Raymond Sebond, is a sustained sceptical attack on human pretensions to knowledge.
