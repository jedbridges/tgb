## Overview

About three hundred pages, an analytical essay by Stendhal, published in 1822, written after his own unrequited passion for Matilde Dembowski in Milan. He distinguishes passionate love, mannered love, physical love and vanity love, and describes the seven stages by which a person falls in love, the second of which he names crystallisation.

Crystallisation is the process by which the mind covers the beloved with perfections as a bare branch left in a salt mine comes out covered with crystals. Later chapters compare love in different countries, discuss the education of women and collect anecdotes and maxims.

## What happens

**Born from unrequited passion.** Stendhal writes this essay after his own unrequited passion for Matilde Dembowski in Milan, transforming personal romantic failure into systematic analytical investigation of love's actual psychological mechanics.

**Distinguishing four types of love.** Stendhal distinguishes passionate love, mannered love, physical love and vanity love as genuinely distinct categories, giving romantic experience a considerably more precise vocabulary than ordinary undifferentiated usage.

**The seven stages.** Stendhal describes the seven stages by which a person falls in love, mapping romantic development as sequential progression rather than sudden, unanalyzable event occurring all at once.

**Crystallisation explained.** The second stage, crystallisation, is the process by which the mind covers the beloved with perfections, Stendhal's most celebrated concept rendered through the vivid image of crystals forming on a bare branch.

**Comparing love across countries.** Later chapters compare love in different countries, extending the essay's core analytical framework into comparative cultural observation about how romantic experience varies across national contexts.

**Education and collected wisdom.** The essay concludes by discussing the education of women and collecting anecdotes and maxims, broadening its scope beyond the core theoretical apparatus into practical accumulated observation.
