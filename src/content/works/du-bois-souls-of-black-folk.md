synopsis: >-
  Fourteen essays on the condition of Black Americans forty years after emancipation, each headed by a bar of a spiritual. Du Bois introduces the idea of double consciousness, the sense of always looking at oneself through the eyes of others, and of the veil that divides the races. He examines the Freedmen's Bureau, criticises Booker T. Washington's policy of accommodation, describes teaching in rural Tennessee and the Black Belt of Georgia, argues for higher education, mourns the death of his infant son and ends with a chapter on the sorrow songs.
