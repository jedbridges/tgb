synopsis: >-
  Dewey's systematic logic, written at seventy-nine. He defines logic as the theory of inquiry, and inquiry as the controlled transformation of an indeterminate situation into a settled one. Logical forms, on this account, are not eternal structures but the forms that inquiry has found to work and has stabilised as rules. The book grounds inquiry biologically and culturally, then treats judgement, propositions, terms, mathematics, and the logic of scientific and of social inquiry, arguing throughout against both formalism and the idea that logic reports the structure of reality.
