synopsis: >-
  Published two years after Wittgenstein's death, and written against his own earlier Tractatus. In numbered remarks rather than chapters, he attacks the assumption that words work by naming things and that meaning is a mental accompaniment of speech. Meaning is use, learned in the practices he calls language games; rule-following cannot be secured by any further interpretation; a private language would have no criterion of correctness; and philosophical problems are confusions to be dissolved rather than theories to be built. Part two, on seeing aspects and the inner life, includes the duck-rabbit and the remark that the human body is the best picture of the soul.
