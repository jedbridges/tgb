synopsis: >-
  Bergson's best known book argues that life is driven by an élan vital, an original impulse that pushes through matter into ever new forms and cannot be reduced to mechanism or to design. He contrasts intellect, which evolved to handle solid bodies and so treats everything as spatial and calculable, with intuition, which grasps duration and life from within. The book examines Darwinian and Lamarckian accounts of evolution, the branching of instinct and intelligence, and the intellect's inability to think becoming.
