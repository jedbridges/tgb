synopsis: >-
  Sartre's systematic existentialism, written in occupied Paris. He divides reality into being-in-itself, the solid given world, and being-for-itself, consciousness, which is nothing in itself and exists only as the negation of what it is not. Because consciousness is not a thing, it is condemned to be free, and the elaborate ways people deny this are what Sartre calls bad faith. Long middle sections analyse the body, the look of other people, shame, love, sadism and masochism as failed attempts to become an object for another, and the book closes on existential psychoanalysis and the useless passion of wanting to be God.
