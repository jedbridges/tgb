Fifty works, and they are the ones a student remembers having read: Don Quixote, War and Peace, the Brothers Karamazov, Middlemarch, Moby-Dick, Montaigne's Essays, Plutarch's Lives, the Wealth of Nations, Democracy in America, Capital, the City of God, the Summa, Gibbon, the Principia, Ulysses, Proust. Some are epic because the story is; some because the argument is; a few, Montaigne and Plutarch, because they are collections that no one is expected to finish and everyone dips into for life.

The programs handle them differently. The novels are assigned whole, usually in the last year, at the rate of a hundred pages a week over a term. The treatises are assigned in selections: no list reads all of the Summa or all of Capital, and the guides on this site say which parts each program takes. Montaigne and Plutarch are read by the essay and by the life.

If you want one epic-length book to begin with, the Quixote is the answer: it is a comedy, it is in short chapters, and it is the book about reading too many books, which is what you are about to do. Tolstoy and George Eliot are the summits of the novel; the Confessions and the Comedy on the long shelf are the way up to them.
