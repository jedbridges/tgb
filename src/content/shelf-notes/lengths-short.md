This is the largest length shelf, three hundred and sixty-five works, more than half the site, and it is the answer to the objection that the great books are too long. They are not, mostly. The Apology, the Crito, the Enchiridion, the Prince, the Manifesto, the Declaration, the Groundwork, Antigone, Oedipus, the Symposium, the Bacchae, Descartes's Meditations, Locke's Second Treatise, the Second Discourse: these are the most assigned works on the site, and every one of them is under a hundred and twenty pages.

The shelf holds every Greek tragedy, most of Plato, all of the founding documents, the shorter treatises of the moderns, the essays, the letters and speeches, and the novellas. Difficulty is unrelated to length here: Kant's Groundwork is sixty pages and among the hardest books on the site, and the Enchiridion is fifty and among the easiest.

If you have a weekend, read a tragedy and a dialogue. If you have an evening, read the Apology or the Manifesto. If you are building a year of reading alone, this shelf and the approachable one together are the syllabus, and the programs' first semesters are drawn almost entirely from them. Use the browse filters to combine length with a theme and the list gets short enough to finish.
