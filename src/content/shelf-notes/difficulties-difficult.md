A hundred and five works, and among them the summits most students come to a great books program to climb: the Republic, the Divine Comedy, Paradise Lost, King Lear, Faust, the Brothers Karamazov, Kant's Groundwork, Aquinas's Summa, Marx's Capital, Aristotle's Physics, Lucretius. These are the books that a first reading does not finish. They are on the lists because a semester in a seminar with a tutor is the right way to read them, and the guides on this site are written with that in mind.

Difficulty here comes from density rather than length. Kant's Groundwork is sixty pages and every sentence carries weight. Lucretius is a poem about atoms in a metre that has to be learned. The Republic changes subject every book and turns out to have been about one thing. Lear is the hardest Shakespeare because it is the bleakest. Dante assumes you know the whole of medieval Florence and the whole of Aquinas, and the notes are half the reading.

Do not start here. Do come here, in the second or third year, after the moderate and demanding shelves have taught the habits: reading with a pencil, reading twice, reading aloud, and reading with someone. Every book on this shelf was written to be argued about, and the argument is where the reward is.
