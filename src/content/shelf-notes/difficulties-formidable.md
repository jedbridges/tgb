Thirty-five works, and the programs that assign them do so in tutorials rather than seminars, a few pages a week, at the board or with a commentary open. Newton's Principia, Kant's Critique of Pure Reason, Hegel's Phenomenology, Aristotle's Metaphysics and Organon, Plotinus, Ptolemy, Copernicus, Kepler, Archimedes, Apollonius, Keynes, Hume's Treatise: these are the books whose difficulty is the subject, not the style.

Most of them are mathematics or philosophy at the point where the argument can only be followed by doing it. The Principia is proofs; you read it with a pencil and a diagram or not at all. The Almagest fits the planets to circles and the fitting is the book. The Critique of Pure Reason builds a system in which every term is defined by every other, so that the first hundred pages are unintelligible until the second hundred make them clear. The Phenomenology is the hardest book on the site by common consent.

These are here because the lists are honest about what the tradition contains, and because a program that reads Euclid in the first year can read Newton in the third. No one should begin with this shelf. Everyone who finishes the others should come to it, and most who do report that the book they feared was the one that mattered.
