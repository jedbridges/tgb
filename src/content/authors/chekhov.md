Anton Chekhov was a grocer's son from Taganrog who supported his family through medical school by writing comic sketches, became the greatest short-story writer in any language, and in his last decade wrote the four plays that made the Moscow Art Theatre: *The Seagull*, *Uncle Vanya*, *Three Sisters* and *The Cherry Orchard*. In them nothing much happens and everything is at stake, and the characters talk past one another about the lives they have not lived. He was a practising doctor, travelled to the penal colony of Sakhalin to report on it, and died of tuberculosis at forty-four in a German spa, after a glass of champagne.
