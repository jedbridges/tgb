Ovid was the wit of Augustan Rome, the poet of love and its arts, who was exiled by Augustus in the year 8 to the Black Sea for, he said, a poem and a mistake, and died there without pardon. The *Metamorphoses*, fifteen books of transformations from the creation to Caesar's deification, gathered the Greek myths into the form in which Europe received them: Daphne, Narcissus, Pygmalion, Orpheus, Icarus. Chaucer, Shakespeare, Titian and Bernini worked from him, and no book after the Bible supplied more subjects to art. The *Amores* and *Art of Love* are the poems that got him into trouble.
