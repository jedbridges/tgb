William Gilbert was physician to Elizabeth I and president of the College of Physicians, and in 1600 published *On the Loadstone and Magnetic Bodies*, the first great work of experimental science in England, which reported eighteen years of experiments with magnets and concluded that the earth itself is a great magnet, which is why the compass points north. Galileo praised him, Kepler took from him the idea of a force acting at a distance, and Bacon, who disliked his speculation, borrowed his method. He coined the word electricity from the Greek for amber. He died of plague three years after the book appeared.
