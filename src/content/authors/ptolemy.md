Ptolemy was a Greek astronomer and geographer working in Alexandria in the second century, about whom nothing personal is known. The *Almagest*, in thirteen books, gathered the astronomy of the Greeks into a mathematical system in which the earth stands still at the centre and the sun, moon and planets circle it on epicycles and eccentrics that reproduce their observed motions, and it was the astronomy of the world for fourteen centuries, until Copernicus. His *Geography* mapped the known world, and his *Tetrabiblos* the astrology. St. John's reads the *Almagest* in the sophomore mathematics tutorial, before Copernicus and Kepler, to see what they overturned.
