Edmund Burke was an Irish-born member of Parliament who spent thirty years in opposition, prosecuting Warren Hastings for the misgovernment of India and defending the American colonies, and who then, in *Reflections on the Revolution in France*, wrote the founding statement of modern conservatism: that a society is a partnership between the living, the dead and the unborn, and that abstract rights are no substitute for inherited liberties. Wollstonecraft and Paine answered him within months. *A Philosophical Enquiry*, written in his twenties, gave the eighteenth century its theory of the sublime.
