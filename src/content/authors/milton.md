John Milton was a Cambridge-educated poet who set aside verse for twenty years to write pamphlets for the Puritan cause, defending divorce, a free press in *Areopagitica*, and the execution of the king, and served as Latin secretary to Cromwell's government. Blind by 1652 and in danger after the Restoration, he dictated *Paradise Lost*, the English epic of the fall, and followed it with *Paradise Regained* and *Samson Agonistes*. Johnson thought no one ever wished it longer; the lists assign it anyway, and Yale's Directed Studies reads it as the summit of English Renaissance poetry.
