Apollonius of Perga was the great geometer of the generation after Archimedes, working in Alexandria around 200 BCE, whose *Conics*, in eight books of which seven survive, gave the ellipse, parabola and hyperbola their names and worked out their properties so completely that nothing was added until Descartes. Kepler needed the ellipse for the planets and Newton needed the whole treatise for the *Principia*, and St. John's reads it in the mathematics tutorial for that reason. Almost nothing is known of his life, and the last book was lost by the tenth century.
