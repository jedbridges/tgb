Matthew Arnold was an inspector of schools for thirty-five years and, in the time left over, the most influential critic of the Victorian age and one of its finest poets. *Dover Beach* and *The Scholar-Gipsy* are in every anthology; the *Poems* the lists assign include them. *The Study of Poetry*, with its touchstones and its claim that poetry would replace religion, and *Culture and Anarchy*, with its Hebraism and Hellenism, set the terms of English criticism for a century. His father was headmaster of Rugby, and he never quite escaped the school.
