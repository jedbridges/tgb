Willa Cather grew up on the Nebraska prairie among Bohemian and Scandinavian immigrants, worked as a journalist and editor in Pittsburgh and New York, and in her forties began the novels of the plains that made her name. *My Ántonia* is the memory of an immigrant girl by the boy who loved her; *A Lost Lady* the portrait of a pioneer's widow in a declining town. She wrote in a plain, exact prose against the fashions of the twenties, and her later books turned to the Southwest and Quebec. She asked that no letters be published; the ban expired in 2011.
