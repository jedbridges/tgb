Blaise Pascal was a mathematical prodigy who invented a calculating machine at nineteen, founded probability theory with Fermat, and proved the existence of the vacuum against the Aristotelians. After a night of religious ecstasy in 1654, recorded on a paper sewn into his coat, he gave himself to Port-Royal and the Jansenist cause, writing *The Provincial Letters* against the Jesuits. The *Pensées* are the notes for an unfinished defence of Christianity, found in bundles after his death at thirty-nine, and contain the wager, the two infinities, and the thinking reed.
