Al-Ghazali was the most celebrated theologian of medieval Islam, professor at the Nizamiyya college in Baghdad at thirty-three, who suffered a crisis in 1095, lost the power of speech, gave up his post, and wandered for ten years as a Sufi. *The Rescuer from Error* is his account of that crisis and of his examination of the theologians, philosophers, esotericists and mystics, ending in the certainty he found in Sufi experience. *The Incoherence of the Philosophers* attacked Avicenna and Aristotle; *The Revival of the Religious Sciences* rebuilt Islamic practice around inward devotion. He is often compared to Augustine.
