Michel de Montaigne was a Gascon nobleman and magistrate who retired at thirty-eight to the tower of his château to read and write, and invented the essay, the trial of one's own thoughts on a subject. The *Essays*, on cannibals, friendship, cruelty, experience, and above all himself, grew through three editions between 1580 and his death, becoming more personal and more sceptical, with the question what do I know as their motto. He was mayor of Bordeaux and a go-between in the wars of religion. Shakespeare read him in Florio, Pascal argued with him, and every essayist since has been his descendant.
