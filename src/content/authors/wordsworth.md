William Wordsworth was a Cumberland lawyer's son who walked through revolutionary France, fathered a daughter there, and returned to write, with Coleridge, the *Lyrical Ballads* of 1798 that began English Romanticism, with its Preface on the language of common men and poetry as emotion recollected in tranquillity. The *Poems* of 1807 contain the Immortality Ode and the sonnets; *The Prelude*, the autobiography of his own mind, was published after his death. He lived at Grasmere and Rydal with his sister Dorothy, grew conservative, became Poet Laureate at seventy-three, and outlived the poets who had mocked him.
