François Rabelais was a Franciscan and then a Benedictine monk who left the cloister for medicine, lectured on Hippocrates at Montpellier, practised in Lyons, and published under an anagram of his name the five books of *Gargantua and Pantagruel*, the adventures of two giants, father and son, through education, war, marriage and a voyage to the oracle of the Bottle. The books are learned, obscene and enormous, a comedy of appetite in a language of invented words, and the Sorbonne condemned each as it appeared. The abbey of Thélème, whose rule is do what you will, is the humanist ideal in a sentence. He died a parish priest.
