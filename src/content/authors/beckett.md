Samuel Beckett left Dublin for Paris, worked for Joyce, fought with the Resistance, and after the war wrote in French, he said, because it was easier to write without style. *Waiting for Godot*, in which two tramps wait by a tree for someone who never comes, was performed in 1953 and changed the theatre; *Endgame*, *Krapp's Last Tape* and the trilogy of novels followed, each with less. He won the Nobel Prize in 1969 and gave the money away. His last works are a few pages long, and the last words of *The Unnamable* are: I can't go on, I'll go on.
