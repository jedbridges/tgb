Thomas à Kempis was a canon regular of the Congregation of Windesheim, in the Low Countries, who spent seventy years in the monastery of Mount St Agnes copying manuscripts and writing devotional works. *The Imitation of Christ*, generally though not universally attributed to him, appeared around 1418 and is the most read Christian book after the Bible: four short books of counsel on the inner life, contempt of the world, the consolation of Christ and the sacrament, in a plain Latin that asks what it profits to dispute learnedly about the Trinity if one lacks humility. Ignatius, Wesley and George Eliot's Maggie Tulliver all read it.
