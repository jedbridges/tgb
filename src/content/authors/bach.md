Johann Sebastian Bach was cantor of St Thomas's church in Leipzig for the last twenty-seven years of his life, responsible for the music of four churches and the school choir, and wrote a cantata for nearly every Sunday. The *St Matthew Passion* of 1727, for two choirs and two orchestras, is the setting of the Gospel's account of the crucifixion that Mendelssohn revived in 1829 and that St. John's reads with the score in the senior music tutorial. He was known in his lifetime as an organist; the world found out he was the greatest composer in the century after his death.
