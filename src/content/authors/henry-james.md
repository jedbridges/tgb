Henry James, younger brother of the philosopher, left America for Europe in his thirties and became the novelist of the encounter between the two: innocent Americans and worldly Europeans, in *The American*, *The Portrait of a Lady* and, at the end, *The Ambassadors*, which he thought his best. His late style, long, qualified and inward, divides readers to this day. *The Beast in the Jungle* is the great story of a life spent waiting for something that never comes. He became a British subject in 1915 in protest at American neutrality and died the next year.
