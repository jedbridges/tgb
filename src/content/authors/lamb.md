Charles Lamb was a clerk at the East India House for thirty-three years who cared for his sister Mary after she killed their mother in a fit of madness, and who wrote, as Elia, the most loved familiar essays in English, on roast pig, old china and the South Sea House. *On the Tragedies of Shakespeare* argues that *Lear* cannot be acted, that the plays are greater in the reading than on the stage, a claim every generation since has tested. He and Mary wrote the *Tales from Shakespeare* for children. His friends were Coleridge, Wordsworth and Hazlitt, and his Wednesday evenings were the centre of literary London.
