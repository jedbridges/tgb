Niccolò Machiavelli served the Florentine republic for fourteen years as secretary and diplomat, was dismissed, tortured and exiled to his farm when the Medici returned in 1512, and wrote *The Prince* that winter to win their favour. It failed, and made his name a byword for cunning. The *Discourses on Livy*, longer and less read, show the republican he really was, and the two together founded political science on how men behave rather than how they should. He wrote comedies, a history of Florence and letters, and told a friend that in the evenings he put on court dress to converse with the ancients.
