Francis Beaumont and John Fletcher wrote together for about six years, from around 1607, sharing lodgings, a mistress and, Aubrey says, clothes, and produced the tragicomedies that dominated the Jacobean stage after Shakespeare retired. *Philaster*, a romance of a disguised page, a wronged prince and a happy ending after threatened tragedy, set the pattern. Beaumont married and stopped writing in 1613 and died three years later; Fletcher went on alone and with Massinger and Shakespeare, with whom he wrote *Henry VIII* and *The Two Noble Kinsmen*, and died of plague in 1625. The folio of 1647 put their names together for good.
