Philip Massinger was the son of a servant of the Earl of Pembroke who wrote for the London stage for thirty years, in collaboration with Fletcher and then as the King's Men's chief dramatist after Fletcher's death. *A New Way to Pay Old Debts* of 1625, in which the extortioner Sir Giles Overreach is cheated out of his gains and goes mad, was the one Jacobean comedy that never left the repertory, Overreach being a part that Kean and every later actor of villains wanted. He was found dead in bed in 1640 and buried in Fletcher's grave.
