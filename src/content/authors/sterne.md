Laurence Sterne was a Yorkshire parson who, at forty-six, published the first two volumes of *Tristram Shandy* and became the toast of London. The novel, which takes nine volumes to get its hero past his third year, with digressions, black pages, marbled pages and a squiggle for a flourish of a stick, is the first to make its own telling the subject. *A Sentimental Journey*, published weeks before his death from tuberculosis in 1768, gave the age the word sentimental. He was preached against and imitated everywhere, and the modernists claimed him as a founder.
