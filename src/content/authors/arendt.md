Hannah Arendt studied with Heidegger and Jaspers, fled Germany in 1933 and France in 1941, and became in New York the century's most searching political thinker. *The Origins of Totalitarianism* traced Nazism and Stalinism to antisemitism, imperialism and the collapse of the nation-state; *Eichmann in Jerusalem* coined the banality of evil and cost her friends; *The Human Condition* set action against labour and work. The essays in *Crises of the Republic* and *Personal Responsibility Under Dictatorship* apply the thinking to Vietnam, civil disobedience and the question of what an individual owes under a criminal regime.
