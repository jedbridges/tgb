Benvenuto Cellini was a Florentine goldsmith and sculptor, maker of the salt cellar for Francis I and the bronze Perseus in the Piazza della Signoria, who killed at least two men, was imprisoned in the Castel Sant'Angelo and escaped over the walls, and dictated his *Autobiography* to a boy in his workshop in his late fifties. It is the most boastful, violent and alive of Renaissance self-portraits, in which popes, kings, rivals and demons are all met with the same self-regard, and it lay unpublished until 1728. Goethe translated it, and Berlioz made it an opera.
