C. H. Waddington was a British embryologist and geneticist, professor at Edinburgh, who introduced the epigenetic landscape and canalisation, images of how a developing organism is channelled toward its form, and genetic assimilation, by which an acquired response can be taken over by the genes. He was a Marxist in the thirties, an operational researcher in the war, and a theorist of biology's relation to art and ethics after it. *The Nature of Life* of 1961 is his short account of what biology had learned and still could not explain, and the Great Books set added it as the biology of the age.
