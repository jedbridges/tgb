James Boswell was a Scottish laird's son who came to London at twenty-two, kept the *London Journal* of his adventures, met Johnson in a bookshop the next year, and made recording him his life's work. The *Life of Samuel Johnson* of 1791, built from twenty years of notes, is the first modern biography and the greatest in English, preserving Johnson's conversation as no one else's has been preserved. Boswell was a lawyer, a drunk, a chaser of women and a man of touching honesty about all three, and the journals found at Malahide Castle in the twentieth century made him a subject in his own right.
