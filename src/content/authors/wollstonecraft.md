Mary Wollstonecraft ran a school, was a governess, and joined the radical publisher Joseph Johnson's circle in London, where she answered Burke's *Reflections* within a month in *A Vindication of the Rights of Men* and, two years later, extended the argument to her own sex in *A Vindication of the Rights of Woman*, the founding text of feminism. She went to Paris to watch the Revolution, had a daughter by an American adventurer, twice attempted suicide, married William Godwin, and died giving birth to the daughter who became Mary Shelley. Godwin's candid memoir destroyed her reputation for a century.
