Jean-Jacques Rousseau, a watchmaker's son from Geneva, arrived in Paris at thirty without a profession and became, through a prize essay arguing that the arts had corrupted morals, the most disputed writer in Europe. The *Discourse on Inequality* traced society's evils to property; the *Social Contract* proposed a state founded on the general will; *Emile* reimagined education from the child's side. Both were burned in Paris and Geneva in 1762, and he spent his last years in flight and in the *Confessions*. The Revolution claimed him, and so did the Romantics.
