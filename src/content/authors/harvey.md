William Harvey studied at Padua under Fabricius, became physician to James I and Charles I, and in 1628 published, in a slim Latin book, the demonstration that the blood circulates, pumped by the heart through the arteries and returning through the veins, against fifteen centuries of Galen. *On the Motion of the Heart and Blood* is the founding text of experimental physiology and the reason the Great Books set puts him beside Galileo. *On the Circulation* answers his critics; *On the Generation of Animals*, from his old age, holds that every animal comes from an egg.
