Mary Shelley was the daughter of Wollstonecraft and Godwin, eloped with Percy Shelley at sixteen, and wrote *Frankenstein* at eighteen on Lake Geneva in the wet summer of 1816, when Byron challenged the party to write ghost stories. The novel, of a student who makes a living creature and abandons it, was published anonymously in 1818, and the creature's own account of his education and rejection is its centre. Shelley drowned in 1822; she lost three of four children, edited his poems, wrote five more novels, and supported herself and her son by her pen. *The Last Man* imagines the end of the human race.
