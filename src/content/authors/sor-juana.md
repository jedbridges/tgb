Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz was a Mexican prodigy who read at three, asked to be sent to the university disguised as a boy, served at the viceroy's court, and at nineteen entered a convent because, she said, it was the least unsuitable way to live for one who wished to study. She wrote plays, love poems, philosophical verse and the *First Dream*, and in the *Reply to Sor Filotea* defended a woman's right to learning against the bishop who had rebuked her. Pressed by her confessor, she sold her library of four thousand books and died nursing her sisters in a plague. She is the first great poet of the Americas.
