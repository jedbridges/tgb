Alfred North Whitehead was a Cambridge mathematician who wrote *Principia Mathematica* with his former pupil Russell, moved to London to teach applied mathematics and think about education, and at sixty-three accepted a chair in philosophy at Harvard, where he built the process metaphysics of *Science and the Modern World* and *Process and Reality*. *The Aims of Education* collects his addresses to teachers, and *Adventures of Ideas* is the summary of his thought a general reader can follow. He was among the most learned and least dogmatic philosophers of his century, and the lists assign him as the last of the great system builders.
