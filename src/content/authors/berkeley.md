George Berkeley was an Irish clergyman and later bishop of Cloyne who, in his twenties, argued that matter does not exist: to be is to be perceived, and the world consists of ideas in minds, held in being by God. The *Principles of Human Knowledge* makes the case and the *Three Dialogues* make it readable. He spent three years in Rhode Island waiting for funds for a college in Bermuda that never came, wrote on tar-water as a cure for everything, and is the middle term between Locke and Hume in every history of British empiricism. Johnson refuted him by kicking a stone.
