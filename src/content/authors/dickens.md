Charles Dickens worked in a blacking factory at twelve when his father was jailed for debt, became a parliamentary reporter, and at twenty-four had London laughing at *The Pickwick Papers*. Fourteen novels followed, published in monthly parts to an audience that waited at the docks for the next number. *David Copperfield* reworks his own youth; *Hard Times* attacks the political economists; *Little Dorrit* returns to the debtors' prison. He edited magazines, gave public readings that wore him out, and died at fifty-eight with a novel unfinished. No English novelist has been read by more people.
