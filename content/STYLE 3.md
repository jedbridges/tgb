# Style guide for reading guides

**Who it's for.** A smart 19-year-old the week before seminar. They will read the book; the guide helps them read it well.

**Voice.** Confident, plain, specific. Say what happens and what is at stake. No throat-clearing, no "timeless masterpiece", no "delve", no "tapestry", no "in conclusion". Prefer a concrete detail to an adjective. Short sentences are fine. Contractions are fine. No em dashes (use commas, colons, or a new sentence).

**Lengths.**
- `synopsis`: 90-120 words. What the book is and what happens or what it argues.
- `whyItMatters`: 100-160 words. Why programs keep assigning it; what conversation it started or changed.
- Body: 700-1100 words, these headings in this order:
  - `## Overview` (what kind of book it is, how it is built, what to expect)
  - `## What happens` for narrative and drama, or `## The argument` for philosophy, theology, science, political thought (walk through the structure: books, parts, acts)
  - `## How to read it` (practical: where to start, what to skip on a first read, what to watch for, common misreadings, which translation and why)
  - `## Questions it raises` (4-6 seminar-style questions, as a bulleted list, each a real question the text leaves open)
- `keyThemes`: 3-5 entries; each `note` is one or two sentences about how *this* book treats that theme.
- `highlights`: 3-5 passages. Prefer public-domain translations or the original with your own literal rendering; if quoting a modern copyrighted translation keep it under 40 words and name the translator. Never invent a quotation. If you are not certain of the wording, paraphrase and add `note: "Paraphrase."` with the location.
- `recommendedEdition`: one, with a one-line reason. `otherEditions`: up to two, each with a reason.

**Accuracy.** Names, dates, book/chapter numbers must be right. If unsure, be vaguer rather than wrong.

**Status.** Write as `status: draft`. A human review bumps to `reviewed`, then `published`.
